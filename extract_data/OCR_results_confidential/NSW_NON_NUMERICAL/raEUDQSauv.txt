Academic Grade

The grade listed in the semester report each year, summarises achievement in selected course components. These will be a
subset of the total skills and knowledge assessed by the end of the school year. The grade on this report may therefore not be
indicative of the final grade to be received after a full year of assessment.

Student achievement is measured against standards, rather than against the achievement of other students. In standards
referenced assessment, the number of students who are awarded any one grade in a subject may vary from semester to
semester, according to the quality of student work. In addition, the number of students who are awarded particular grades (eg:
A, B, C, D or E) will vary from subject to subject.

The following terms describe the Academic Grade given for the level of achievement of all knowledge and skills in each course:

A The student demonstrates extensive knowledge of content and understanding of course concepts, and applies highly
developed skills and processes in a wide variety of contexts. In addition the student demonstrates creative and critical
hinking skills using perceptive analysis and evaluation. The student effectively communicates complex ideas and
information.
B The student demonstrates thorough knowledge of content and understanding of course concepts, and applies well-
developed skills and processes in a variety of contexts. In addition the student demonstrates creative and critical
hinking skills using analysis and evaluation. The student clearly communicates complex ideas and information.

c The student demonstrates sound knowledge of content and understanding of course concepts, and applies skills and

processes in a range of familiar contexts. In addition the student demonstrates skills in selecting and integrating

information and communicates relevant ideas in an appropriate manner.

D The student demonstrates a basic knowledge of content and understanding of course concepts, and applies skills and

processes in some familiar contexts. In addition the student demonstrates skills in selecting and using information and
communicates ideas in a descriptive manner.

E The student demonstrates an elementary knowledge of content and understanding of course concepts, and applies
some skills and processes with guidance. In addition the student demonstrates elementary skills in recounting
information and communicating ideas.

Course Outcomes
The following terms describe the level of competency achieved against each outcome of the course.
Extensive, Thorough, Sound, Basic, Modified and Life Skills.

Biology Mrs J Duncan

In the first Biology module on Heredity, students reviewed processes of sexual and asexual reproduction and their significance
in ensuring the continuity of species. The structure of DNA, its importance in being replicated correctly during cell division and its
critical role in directing protein synthesis have also been studied. Students have investigated a range of inheritance patterns and
have used these to predict expected characteristics in offspring, extending this learning to population genetics. In the second
module on Genetic Change, students have considered mutagens, how these cause a range of mutations and the effect of these
on genetic material. The use of biotechnology and various genetic technologies to induce genetic change in medical, industrial
and agricultural applications have been extensively studied with consideration of their impact on Earth's biodiversity, their
potential to alter DNA permanently and the benefits, issues and ethics associated with the use of genetic modification. Practical
investigations have been undertaken throughout the semester to reinforce course content and enhance working scientifically
skills, including the first assessment task which investigated the interaction between genes and the environment.

Physics Mr M Eichler

With the commencement of the Higher School Certificate programme, the students this semester have studied the course
modules of Advanced Mechanics and Electromagnetism. In Advanced Mechanics, students developed an understanding that all
forms of complex motion can be understood by analysing the forces acting on a system, including the energy transformations
taking place within and around the system. By applying new mathematical techniques, students modelled and predicted the
motion of objects. They examined two-dimensional motion, including projectile motion and uniform circular motion, along with
the orbital motion of planets and satellites, which were modelled as an approximation to uniform circular motion.

The understanding gained in Advanced Mechanics was applied in a Depth Study investigating the physics of model rockets. In
teams, students analysed the physics involved in predicting the height and time of flight of a model rocket. They then conducted
an experiment to test their predictions and compared their model to the actual data. This experiment and analysis of the results
was assessed.

In Electromagnetism students examined the similarities and differences in the interactions of single charges in electric and
magnetic fields. Phenomena that include the force produced on a current-carrying wire in a magnetic field, the force between
current-carrying wires, Faraday’s Law of Electromagnetic Induction, the principles of transformers and the workings of motors
and generators were investigated. The role of the law of conservation of energy in these situations was also explored.

Advanced English Mrs S Kelly

In order to enhance their personal, social and vocational lives, the Advanced English course aims to help students develop
critical and sophisticated language skills and become thoughtful, imaginative and effective communicators in a diverse and
changing world. This semester, students engaged with Module A: Textual Conversations, in which they considered the
connections between Shakespeare’s ‘The Tempest’ and its evolution into Margaret Atwood’s ‘Hag-seed.’ Following this, they
completed a study of Module C: The Craft of Writing, which challenged them to develop agility in their writing and apply a keen
critical eye when reading a variety of texts. Through an examination of these prescribed texts, and other related texts, students
considered the ways in which language is used in increasingly sophisticated ways to communicate ideas that affect readers,
listeners and viewers. The semester concluded with the Trial Examination, feedback and revision.

Biology Mrs J Duncan

In the third Biology module on Infectious Disease, students examined the cause, transmission, prevention, treatment and control
of a range of infectious diseases in local, regional and global arenas. The human immune system and its response in protecting
individuals from infectious disease has been studied as well as herd immunity and vaccination through a formal Depth Study.
Disease prevention and management was further extended to include various medical and agricultural applications. In the final
module on Non-infectious Disease and Disorders, students considered causes, effects, incidence, prevalence and mortality of a
range of non-infectious diseases in humans. They explored specific technologies in treating disorders like hearing loss, visual
defects and kidney issues. The purpose and benefits of epidemiological studies to human health, in addition to prevention of non
-infectious diseases via educational programs and genetic engineering were examined. Practical and research investigations
have been undertaken throughout the semester to reinforce course content and enhance working scientifically skills. Student
knowledge, understanding and skills have been assessed through a Vaccination Depth Study and a Higher School Certificate
Trial Examination.

Physics Mr M Eichler

This semester, Physics students have studied the course modules of The Nature of Light and From the Universe to the Atom. In
the module The Nature of Light, students investigated the changing ideas about what light is and the conflicting evidence
available. They developed an understanding that light must be both a wave and a particle, the dual-nature of light. The
consequences of the absolute value for the speed of light is examined further in terms of the Special Theory of Relativity.

In the module From the Universe to the Atom, students investigate how our understanding of the nature of the atom has
developed with the discovery of new information over time. This ranges from nucleosynthesis occurring after the formation of the
universe, to within stars, through to the Standard Model of matter and the world of quantum mechanics. Students have
completed a two-hour practical test assessing a range of skills and concepts from modules Electromagnetism (covered last
term), The Nature of Light and From the Universe to the Atom. They have also completed a Higher School Certificate Trial
Examination.

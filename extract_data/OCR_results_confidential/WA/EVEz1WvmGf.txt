School Values

Japanese as a Second Language ATAR ANGLICAN SCHOOL FOR GIRLS

Teacher: Dr S. Ainsworth

Working within the themes of the individual, the Japanese speaking communities and the changing world,
students acquired the skills necessary to talk in depth about themselves as young travellers overseas and in
Japan. Through the focus topics of travel and part time jobs, students explored issues and described travel
preparations including; what to take, booking accommodation, transport options, local attractions, tales of
travel and described part time work. Students gained deeper cultural understandings through comparing
and contrasting life in Japan and Australia, both traditional and modern. They also examined typical
problems impacting the success of a trip, such as lost property and staying healthy. Students also had the
opportunity to improve their oral skills by participating in weekly oral sessions with language assistant staff.

Mathematics Methods ATAR ANGLICAN SCHOOL FOR GIRLS

Teacher: Mr C. Croft

Students have continued their study of calculus by establishing the derivatives of exponential and
trigonometric functions and their applications, as well as some basic differentiation techniques and the
concept of a second derivative, its meaning and applications. Students can employ integration, both as a
process that reverses differentiation and as a way of calculating areas, and can use the fundamental
theorem of calculus as a link between differentiation and integration. Students have been introduced to
discrete random variables and can model random processes involving chance and variation, with a purpose
to develop a framework for statistical inference.

Laws of Motion and the gravitational field model to analyse motion on inclined planes, the motion of
projectiles, and satellite motion. They have also developed an understanding of electromagnetic
interactions and applied this knowledge to the operation of direct current motors.

Students have developed this understanding through contexts and investigations of motion and
electromagnetic phenomena. They continue to develop skills in planning, conducting and interpreting the
results of primary and secondary investigations.

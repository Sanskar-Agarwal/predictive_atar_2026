Year 11 Physics - Subject Overview

At the core of all scientific endeavour is the inquiry into the nature of the universe. Scientists recognise that
knowledge is not fixed, but is always fallible and open to challenge. As such, scientific endeavour is never
conducted in isolation, but builds on and challenges an existing body of knowledge in the pursuit of more
reliable knowledge. This collaborative process, whereby new knowledge is gained, is essential to the
cooperative advancement of science, technology, health and society in the 21st century.

Physics provides opportunities for students to engage with the classical and modern understandings of the
universe. In Semester 1, students engage with the concept of gravitational and electromagnetic fields, and the
relevant forces associated with them. Finally, in Semester 2, students study modern physics theories and
models that, despite being counterintuitive, are fundamental to our understanding of many common
observable phenomena.

Evidence of student progress is gathered from: a research report and an exam in Semester 1; an extended
experimental investigation and two exams in Semester 2. Each item will consist of all three criteria as outline
in the QCAA Physics Syllabus (2007). Each item contributes equally to a student’s overall level of
achievement.

Year 11 Mathematics B - Subject Overview

Grades for Year 11 Mathematics A, B and C reports are based on the categories of general objectives of each
of these courses. These are detailed in the relevant syllabus documents (2014 amendment). These categories
of objectives are: Knowledge and Procedures, Modelling and Problem Solving, and Communication and
Justification.

Knowledge and Procedures involves students demonstrating the recall and use of results and mathematical
procedures. Modelling and Problem Solving involves the uses of mathematics in which students model
mathematical situations, solve problems and undertake mathematical investigations. Communication and
Justification involves the presentation, communication, logical argument, interpretation, justification of
mathematics and checking the reasonableness of answers or results.

Evidence of student progress is gathered from a summative test at the end of each term, and an Extended
Modelling and Problem Solving Task each semester. All assessment items assess all three criteria. The
current report grade comes from an equal contribution of each criterion. Each criterion is given an individual
grade from A+ to E. The cumulative grade is a combination of semester 1 and 2 current grades.

Year 11 Physics - Subject Overview

Physics provides opportunities for students to engage with the classical and modern understandings of the
universe. Students study Physics for four periods per week. In Semester 1, students learn about the
fundamental concepts of thermodynamics, electricity and nuclear processes. In Semester 2, students learn
about the concepts and theories that predict and describe the linear motion of objects. Further, they will
explore how scientists explain some phenomena using an understanding of waves.

Evidence of student progress is gathered from: a student research report and an exam in Semester 1; a
student extended experimental investigation and two exams in Semester 2. Each item will consist of all three
criteria as outline in the QCAA Physics Syllabus (2007) — Knowledge and Conceptual Understanding;
Investigative Processes; and, Evaluating and Concluding. Each item contributes equally to a student’s overall
level of achievement in Physics.

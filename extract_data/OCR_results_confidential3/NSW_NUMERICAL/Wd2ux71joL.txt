PERSONAL STATEMENT

The origins of the cosmos is one of the most mysterious phenomena in physics. I remember my
excitement when I researched about the Big Bang Theory and learnt the intricacies of special
relativity. Iam fascinated by the formation of stars, planets and life, and the nature of space-time
itself. Accompanying this passion is my firm interest in the mechanics behind computerised
systems. My projects include planning three-way switches, building a binary calculator from
marbles and cardboard, and coding a 600+ line Processing game in Java. I rapidly absorb maps,
circuits and diagrams - resulting in my joking nickname, the 'human compass.' My strong physics
skills and creativity led me to the field of engineering. USYD's Space Engineering course
perfectly combines my passions and strengths. The notion that I may be able to use some of my
own skills in physics, computing, creating, to further our scientific knowledge, or to discover new
insights into the universe, is incredibly exciting.

KEY ATTRIBUTES

1. Curious and creative problem solver. From three-way switches to one-sided paper,
intrigue has prompted me to understand the world not through research, but redesign - and in
increasingly creative and challenging ways.

2. Strong work ethic and the motivation to succeed. This is seen in my academic success
and my art major work. My third, sculptural element had to be illustrated, laser-etched, inked,
sanded, glued and mounted. By dividing these tasks into 11 stages and 51 individual goals, I
completed my project in 4 days.

3. Extensive teamwork and time management skills. From debating to da vinci to netball,
I've worked successfully in groups in many outcomes. My success lies in my ability to see the big
picture, my calm mentality and goal-driven vision.

FUTURE GOALS

My father worked in telecommunications, his career spanning from electro-mechanical switches
to modern computerized systems. During my journey from childhood to senior physics, I
badgered him (to varying degrees of success) about everything from ground wires to fax
machines.

Inspired by my father's knowledge, my aim after pursuing space engineering at university is to
use my skills to advance the next revolution in space travel and science. My goal is not to be the
astronaut inside the spaceship, but the engineer-physicist who designs the rocket launcher and
software to explore space and bring the astronaut home.

I aspire to work in a space agency or company, building the spacecraft and technology to help us
enter space. I hope my career will exploit my creativity, problem solving, and passions for
computing and space. In the nearer future, I aim to extend myself academically at university. I
hope to continue debating, and I would absolutely love to work on the Silvereye project!

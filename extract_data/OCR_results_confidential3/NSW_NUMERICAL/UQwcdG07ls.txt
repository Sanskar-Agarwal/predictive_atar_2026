How to Read this Academic Report

The Academic Report presents a detailed analysis of a student’s achievement and application for each course

* levels of achievement for course outcomes

* an Examination Mark and the Examination Median (if applicable) and Position in Course
* an indicator of overall effort

* teacher’s comment, including areas for improvement.

Student Course Outcomes

At Hills Grammar we report information on student progress in terms of student course outcomes. A student
outcome, plainly speaking, is something that the student knows, understands or can do. Our teachers, like all
educators in NSW, design their educational programs in line with outcomes specified and mandated by the NSW
Education Standards Authority (NESA) syllabus documents. Assessment tasks and examinations test specific
outcomes. It makes sense, then, to report on the level of achievement the students have attained against these
outcomes. The following descriptors are to be used when interpreting a student’s achievement of Course
Outcomes:

Extensive: | Students have achieved the outcome at an extensive level for the stage. They demonstrate breadth
and depth in their knowledge and understanding of the outcome and have a very high level of
proficiency in the processes and skills for the stage.

High: Students have achieved the outcome at a thorough level for the stage. They consistently
demonstrate a high level of knowledge and understanding of the outcome and proficiency in the
processes and skills for the stage.

Sound: Students have achieved the outcome at a sound level for the stage. They demonstrate sound
knowledge and understanding of the outcome and proficiency in the processes and skills for the
stage.

Developing: | Students have achieved the outcome at a basic level for the stage. They demonstrate a developing
level of knowledge and understanding of the outcome and some proficiency in the processes and
skills for the stage.

Elementary: | Students at this level are working at an elementary level with the outcome for the stage. They
demonstrate an elementary level of knowledge and understanding of the outcome and a very limited
proficiency in the processes and skills for the stage. They may need guidance and teacher support
in order to demonstrate achievement of the outcomes and to increase consistency of performance.

Marks and Grades for Academic Reports

The course Marks and Course Median (middle mark) provided in Academic Reports, give an overall numeric
summary (Years 7-11 only) of a student's achievement in relation to the syllabus outcomes being assessed.
Course Marks are based on all assessment tasks completed at the time of reporting. The table below lists the
general achievement descriptors for Year 11:

A: The student demonstrates extensive knowledge of content and understanding of course concepts,
and applies highly developed skills and processes in a wide variety of contexts. In addition the
student demonstrates creative and critical thinking skills using perceptive analysis and evaluation.
The student effectively communicates complex ideas and information.

B: The student demonstrates thorough knowledge of content and understanding of course concepts,
and applies well-developed skills and processes in a variety of contexts. In addition the student
demonstrates creative and critical thinking skills using analysis and evaluation. The student clearly
communicates complex ideas and information.

C: The student demonstrates sound knowledge of content and understanding of course concepts, and
applies skills and processes in a range of familiar contexts. In addition the student demonstrates
skills in selecting and integrating information and communicates relevant ideas in an appropriate
manner.

D: The student demonstrates a basic knowledge of content and understanding of course concepts, and
applies skills and processes in some familiar contexts. In addition the student demonstrates skills in
selecting and using information and communicates ideas in a descriptive manner.

E: The student demonstrates an elementary knowledge of content and understanding of course
concepts, and applies some skills and processes with guidance. In addition the student
demonstrates elementary skills in recounting information and communicating ideas.

Examination marks and course marks are adjusted to reflect NESA performance bands and assigned cut-offs for
the A - E scale. More information about the determination of marks and grades is discussed in 'Assessment and
Reporting 7-12' on the School Intranet (Academic Programs).

educators in NSW, design their educational programs in line with outcomes specified and mandated by the NESA
syllabus documents. Assessment tasks and examinations test specific outcomes. It makes sense, then, to report
on the level of achievement the students have attained against these outcomes. The following descriptors are to

Extensive: | Students have achieved the outcome at an extensive level for the stage. They demonstrate breadth

High: Students have achieved the outcome at a thorough level for the stage. They consistently

Course Outline

During this semester, students studied two modules, Advanced Mechanics and Electromagnetism. In the unit on Advanced
Mechanics students develop an understanding that all forms of complex motion can be understood by analysing the forces
acting on a system, including the energy transformations taking place within and around the system. By applying new
mathematical techniques, students model and predict the motion of objects within systems. Electromagnetism takes a closer
look at the similarities and differences in the interactions of single charges in electric and magnetic fields providing students
with a conceptual foundation for this module. Phenomena studied include the force produced on a current-carrying wire in a
magnetic field, the force between current-carrying wires, Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction, the principles of
transformers and the workings of motors and generators can all be understood as instances of forces acting on moving
charged particles in magnetic fields. The position in course is based on performance in all HSC assessment tasks completed
to date.

Co-curricular Record 2019

The Co-curricular program at Hills Grammar is an integral part of the whole school curriculum. There are
wonderful opportunities for students to develop their skills and interests across a broad range of activities
including Sport, Creative and Spoken Arts, Music, Leadership and Outdoor Education. These activities take place
before school, during lunch and after school with further weekend commitments in some areas. In addition, every
Thursday the Co-curricular Connect period enables students to participate in their mandatory Co-curricular
activity.

Aims of the program are:

* to foster and develop student leadership

* to encourage and develop co-operation, responsibility and 'team' work

* to develop a sense of belonging and school spirit

* to foster good rapport between staff, students and parents

* to inspire students to strive for excellence in all areas of endeavour

* to provide opportunities for parents, ex-parents and ex-students to contribute to the School
* to establish links with the wider community and develop relationships with various groups
* to provide opportunities for students to achieve success in a diverse mix of interests

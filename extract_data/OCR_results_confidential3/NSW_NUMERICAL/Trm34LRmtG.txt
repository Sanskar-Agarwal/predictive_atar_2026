English Advanced

The study of English enables students to take their place as confident, articulate communicators, critical,
imaginative thinkers and active participants in society, to perceive the world from a variety of perspectives and
to value the richness of diversity.

COURSE OUTLINE

Texts and Human Experiences: common module

Students deepen their understanding of how texts represent individual and collective human experiences by
examining human qualities and emotions associated with, or arising from, these experiences. Students
appreciate, explore, interpret, analyse and evaluate the ways language is used to shape these representations
in a range of texts in a variety of forms, exploring how texts may give insight into human behaviour and
motivations. They also consider the role of storytelling throughout time to express and reflect particular lives
and cultures. Text: Orwell Nineteen Eight-Four.

Module A: Textual Conversations: Students explore the ways in which the comparative study of texts can reveal
resonances and dissonances between and within texts, exploring common or disparate issues, values,
assumptions or perspectives, and how these are depicted. Texts: Shakespeare, William, King Richard III,
Pacino, Al, Looking for Richard

Module B: Critical Study of Text: Students develop detailed analytical and critical knowledge, understanding
and appreciation of selected poems from Earth Hour by David Malouf. Central to this study is the close analysis
of the text's construction, content and language to develop students' own rich interpretation of the text, basing
their judgements on detailed evidence drawn from their research and reading.

Module C: Craft of Writing: Students strengthen and extend their knowledge, skills and confidence as writers
writing for a range of authentic audiences and purposes to convey ideas with power and increasing precision.
Students appreciate, examine and analyse challenging short prescribed texts as well as texts from their own

wide reading, as models and stimulus for the development of their own ideas and written expression.

Outcomes

-describe what a student is expected to know, understand and be able to do as a result of
learning in each course

Mark

examinations

Median

Students deepen their understanding of how texts represent individual and collective human experiences by
examining human qualities and emotions associated with, or arising from, these experiences. Students
appreciate, explore, interpret, analyse and evaluate the ways language is used to shape these representations
in a range of texts in a variety of forms, modes and media, exploring how texts may give insight into the
anomalies, paradoxes and inconsistencies in human behaviour and motivations. They consider the role of
storytelling throughout time to express and reflect particular lives and cultures. Text: Orwell Nineteen Eighty-
Four

Module A: Textual Conversations

Students explore the ways in which the comparative study of texts can reveal resonances and dissonances
between and within texts. They consider the ways that a reimagining or reframing of an aspect of a text might
mirror, align or collide with the details of another text, also exploring common or disparate issues, values,
assumptions or perspectives, and how these are depicted. Texts: Shakespeare, William, King Richard Ill,
Pacino, Al, Looking for Richard

Module C: Craft of Writing

Students strengthen and extend their knowledge, skills and confidence as writers writing for a range of
authentic audiences and purposes to convey ideas with power and increasing precision. Students appreciate,
examine and analyse challenging short prescribed texts as well as texts from their own wide reading, as models
and stimulus for the development of their own ideas ans written expression.
